The Order, End to End

One order, from the tap that places it to the receipt in an inbox — what each of the three people does, what the app does in between, and where the money actually moves.

Customer Store Rider PaspasFood
1
Customer

Places the order

Basket, delivery address, landmark if the gate is hard to find, then confirm. The total on that screen — food, delivery fee, any surcharge — is what will be handed over at the door, in cash. Nothing is added afterwards.

What the app does. A first-time customer is asked to verify their mobile number by SMS before the order can go through. The server then recalculates every figure for itself — prices, fees, coupon, delivery zone, stock — rather than trusting what the phone sent, so a tampered-with app cannot name its own price. The store is alerted, and the alert repeats until it is answered, because an order missed on a counter is an order nobody cooks.
2
Store

Accepts, or declines

The store sees the items, the quantities and any notes. Accepting puts the order in their queue; declining frees the customer to order elsewhere straight away.

Money. Accepting records what the store will earn on this order. It is not paid yet — the cash arrives at pickup, from the rider.
Customer can still cancel. Nothing has been cooked. Cancelling here costs nobody anything.
3
Store

Starts preparing, and says how long

Tapping Start Preparing requires a time — 5 to 45 minutes. It is not a formality: the customer's arrival estimate is calculated from it, and a rider is sent to reach the counter as the food finishes rather than to stand and wait.

A revision extends the countdown, it does not collapse it. Falling behind is normal; the store updates the time and the new one counts from the moment it is set, with the customer told immediately. The app stores a duration and when it was set, never a fixed ready-time, so a revision means “another fifteen minutes from now” and not “fifteen minutes from whenever you first said”.
The customer can no longer cancel from the app. Ingredients are committed. Cancelling now goes through the store or support.
4
Rider

Takes the job — while the food is still cooking

The job appears on the board as soon as the store starts preparing, not when the food is ready. The card shows the shop, the drop-off, the distance and what the job pays, so a rider can judge it before accepting.

Money. Accepting holds one PaspasPoint. It is not spent yet: completing the job uses it, and giving the job back for a reason on the list releases it.
Arriving early is expected. If the order is not ready, the rider taps I’m at the store and the counter is told someone is waiting.
5
Rider Store

Hand-over, and the rider pays

The store hands over the order and the rider pays for the food in cash, out of their own pocket. The amount is the food subtotal on the Store Copy, printed under the words RIDER PAYS — never the customer's total, which is a bigger number containing the rider's own wages.

  • Check the bag against the slip before paying. Afterwards a missing item is a conversation, not a correction.
  • The Customer Copy travels in the bag; it is the customer's receipt.
Money. The store is now paid, in full, in cash — before the food has left the shop. PaspasFood takes no commission from it.
6
Rider

On the way

The customer is told the order has been picked up, and can follow the rider on the map, read the landmark they wrote, and message them in the app without either party seeing the other's personal number.

7
Rider Customer

Delivered, and paid

The customer pays the total shown in the app — the figure they agreed to at step 1 — and the rider keeps the difference between that and what they paid the shop. A proof photo is required; the job cannot be completed without one.

Money. The rider has already been paid, out of the cash in their hand, so nothing is owed to them afterwards and there is nothing to withdraw. The held point is used at this moment. The earning is recorded for their history and their reports.
An itemised receipt is emailed to the customer, showing what was ordered and every fee that made up the total.
8
Customer

Rates the store and the rider

A review can only be left by the person who received that order. It is published with the reviewer’s initials — M***** S***** — never their full name and never their photo.

The other paths

When It Does Not Go Like That

Most orders follow the eight steps. These are the ones that do not.

What happened What the platform does
Nobody takes the job The store is prompted after a while and decides whether to cancel or wait. An order is never cancelled automatically — we do not stop a kitchen that is already cooking.
The store cannot fulfil it The store cancels with a reason. The customer is told what it was and is charged nothing. A rider who had already taken the job keeps their point.
The rider gives the job back The point is returned where the job could not be done — order not ready after a long wait, customer unreachable, address wrong, unsafe to continue — and used where they simply changed their mind. The app decides from the reason chosen and says which before they leave the screen.
Nobody is at the door The rider reports it in the app before leaving. Where they went to the address given, tried to reach the customer through the app and waited a reasonable time, PaspasFood reimburses what they paid the shop and pays the delivery fee. The goods stay with the rider.
The customer refuses the order Same reimbursement for the rider. The customer may be asked to pay for the goods, and repeated incidents can suspend the account.
Something is wrong with the food Report it in the app with the order number. The store is responsible for what it sold; we investigate and tell you what we decided.

The binding version of all of this is in the Terms & Conditions — cancellations and failed deliveries in section 8, PaspasPoints in section 7. Fees and limits are set in the app and shown before you confirm.
