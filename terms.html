Pasugo errands. A Pasugo is an errand in which a rider buys goods from a shop that is not on the platform, using their own money, up to a spending limit you set. You pay for the goods actually bought, plus the fees below. Every fee is shown in the app before you confirm, and all of them are paid to the rider.

  • Pasugo fee — for the value of the goods the rider fronts. Where it applies it is the larger of a flat base fee or a percentage of the goods, never both added together.

Fees calculated from your spending limit are quoted as a maximum and recalculated on what the rider actually spent, so you are never charged more than you were shown. A fee resting on a flat minimum does not reduce when less is spent. A rider may not spend more than your limit; raising it is your decision alone, and it raises the fees calculated from it.

These Terms describe how each fee is WORKED OUT, not what it currently costs. The figures in force — the allowances, the rates, the base fee and the percentage — are shown in the app before you confirm an order, and are the ones you are charged. We publish a change to them in the app rather than by amending this document, so that what you are quoted and what you are charged can never be two different things.

6. How Payment Is Handled

PaspasFood operates one of two payment models. The rider-settled model below is the one in force. Both are described because the platform supports both and we may move between them; if we do, we will say so in the app and here before it takes effect, under section 16.

  • Platform-settled. The rider collects payment on delivery and remits it through PaspasFood, which then settles with the business. Amounts owed appear in the wallet in the app.
  • Rider-settled. The rider pays the business for the goods at pickup from their own funds and collects the full amount from the customer on delivery. PaspasFood does not hold, transmit, or take a share of that money at any point; our charge to the rider is for the use of the platform, not a commission on your order.

In both models PaspasFood is the platform connecting you to the business and the rider. We are not the seller of the goods and we are not a payment service. What a customer pays is the same either way, and it is the total shown in the app before the order is placed; the models differ only in who holds the money in between.

7. PaspasPoints (Riders)

Where PaspasPoints are in use, a rider buys points from PaspasFood and one point pays for one job. Points are a prepaid right to use the platform. They are not money, not a deposit, and not a security.

Held, then used. Accepting a job holds one point: it leaves the rider’s available balance, stays visible as held, and has not yet been spent. Completing the job uses it. A job that ends without being completed releases the hold or consumes it according to section 8, and the app tells the rider which happened before they leave the screen.

  • The price per point is set by PaspasFood and is shown in the app before you buy. A change in price does not change the price you already paid.
  • Points are credited only once payment has actually been received and recorded. A request to buy is not a purchase.
  • Points do not expire.
  • Points are not refundable, not transferable, and have no cash value. They cannot be sold or given to another rider and cannot be converted back into money.
  • There is a maximum number of points a rider may hold at one time, shown in the app. This limit exists to cap what a rider has committed to the platform in advance.
  • Where an account is suspended or closed, unused points are refunded — see section 13. Points that have already been used on completed jobs are not.
  • PaspasFood may correct a point balance to fix an error. Every correction is recorded with a reason and the person who made it, and is visible to the rider in the app.

8. Cancellations and Refunds

When you can cancel, and who bears what. What a cancellation costs depends on how far the order has gone, because at each stage somebody else has already spent something.

  • Before the business accepts it. Cancel freely in the app. Nothing has been cooked and no rider has set out. Nothing is charged.
  • Accepted, not yet being prepared. Still cancellable in the app. Nothing is charged.
  • Being prepared. The business has committed ingredients and time, so cancellation is no longer automatic: contact support or the business through the app. Where food has already been made, you may be asked to pay for it.
  • A rider has collected it. The order is on its way and the rider has already paid for it. You can no longer cancel it yourself. It can still be ended by the rider or by PaspasFood where it cannot be completed — see failed delivery below for what happens then, and for what you may be charged if you refuse an order that arrives.

A business may cancel an order it cannot fulfil, and PaspasFood may cancel where an order cannot be completed. In both cases you are charged nothing, and anything you have already paid is returned. An order is never cancelled automatically for sitting unclaimed: the business is asked first.

Because payment is taken in cash on delivery, a refund is normally a question of not being charged rather than money being returned. Where money has changed hands and should not have, contact support; we aim to answer a refund request within three working days and will say what we decided and why.

Where PaspasPoints are in use, the point held against the job is released when the job is cancelled by the customer, by the business, by PaspasFood, or because it could not be fulfilled.

Where the rider gives a job back themselves, whether the point is released or used depends on the reason they select in the app. It is returned where the job could not be completed through no fault of the rider — the business could not provide the order, the order was not ready after a long wait, the customer could not be reached, or the address was wrong or unreachable — and where stopping was the right thing to do, such as an accident or safety concern, a vehicle breakdown, severe weather, a personal emergency, or the customer asking for an item that may not be bought on a Pasugo under section 9.11. It is not returned where the rider simply chose not to continue, such as distance or traffic, or where no reason is selected from the list: the job was unavailable to every other rider while they held it.

Failed delivery after pickup. Under the rider-settled model the rider has already paid the business for the goods before setting out. If the customer then refuses the order, cannot be reached, or is not at the address given, the rider is left holding goods nobody has paid them for.

A rider in that position must report it in the app before leaving the address, and in any case within 24 hours. PaspasFood then reviews what happened, using what the app already records — the calls and messages sent through it, the location and time, the proof photograph, and the chat with the customer.

  • Where the rider did what was asked of them — went to the address given, attempted contact through the app, and waited a reasonable time — PaspasFood reimburses what they paid the business for the goods. The delivery fee for the trip is paid as well.
  • Where they did not — no attempt at contact, a different address, goods bought outside the order, or a report made days later with nothing to review — the loss stays with the rider. We will say which of these applied.
  • The goods themselves remain the rider’s to keep or dispose of in either case. We do not ask for them back, and a reimbursement is not a purchase.

A customer who refuses a delivered order, is unreachable, or gives an address that does not exist may be asked to pay for the goods, and repeated incidents may lead to suspension under section 13. A customer is not charged for a failure that was not theirs — a rider who never arrived, or arrived somewhere else.

Why this is not left to goodwill. The rider is the only party in this arrangement who puts their own money at risk, and they decide whether to accept a job knowing that. A rule that left them to absorb a stranger’s change of mind would make the careful rider and the careless one equally exposed, and would quietly price the job at something other than what it says on the card.
