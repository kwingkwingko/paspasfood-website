Riding for PaspasFood

Everything you need to start earning, in the order you will meet it. If you only read two parts, read how the money works — it is different from most delivery apps — and how to protect yourself before you pay a store.

Step by step

Getting Started

1

Sign up as a rider

Install the app, create an account and choose Rider. You will need a mobile number, your vehicle type, your driver's licence number, a photo of the licence itself and a selfie holding it. Finish the email verification step — an application that stops there reaches us with your documents missing, and it is the most common reason an application appears to vanish.

2

Wait for approval

An admin checks your details before you can accept work. You will not see jobs until your account is approved — that is normal, not a fault.

3

Get PaspasPoints

You need at least one point to accept a job. Request points from the Points screen in the app; an admin credits them to your account.

PaspasPoints

One Point, One Job

This is how PaspasFood earns. There is no commission on anything you deliver.

🎟️

What a point costs

₱8 per point. You can hold up to 40 at a time, so the most you ever have tied up is ₱320.

✅

What it buys

Accepting one job — a food order, or a Pasugo errand. After that, every peso the customer pays for delivery is yours, and on a bigger Pasugo so is the shopping fee.

Points are bought at the PaspasFood office: request the number you want in the app, then bring the money. Requesting is not buying — they are credited once payment has actually been received. They never expire, and they are not refundable, not transferable and have no cash value, so buy what you expect to use. A price change never affects points you have already paid for.

At zero points you cannot accept work. The job board will look empty or every job will refuse to open. That is not a bug — top up from the Points screen and they come back.

Important

How the Money Works

You handle the cash. PaspasFood never touches it.

1

You pay the store

At pickup you pay the shop for the food or goods, in cash, out of your own pocket. Keep enough float on you to cover a job before you accept it.

2

The customer pays you

At the door the customer hands you the full amount — the goods plus the delivery fee. In cash.

3

You keep the difference

You get your money back plus the delivery fee and any tip. No commission is taken, and there is nothing to withdraw later.

Why your Wallet shows ₱0.00

Because you were already paid, at the door, in cash. The Wallet is a record of what you earned, not money waiting for you. There is nothing to withdraw and nothing missing.

On the job

Doing a Delivery

A food order

  1. Accept from the job board (costs 1 point)
  2. Go to the store and pay for the order
  3. Mark it picked up
  4. Deliver, take payment, upload proof

A Pasugo errand

  1. Read the shopping list, the weight and the customer’s estimate
  2. Buy the list — the estimate tells you how much cash to bring
  3. Record what the shop charged, even if it is more. Keep the receipt — the customer pays what you actually spent
  4. Deliver and collect goods + delivery fee + shopping fee

The customer’s figure is an estimate, not a limit. If the shop charges more, you can record the real amount — confirm it once and the customer is notified immediately, and the errand fee is recalculated on what you actually spent. Record what you really paid, never a rounded guess: it is what the customer is billed and what comes back to you at the door.

Pasugo only

The Shopping Fee

Paid on top of the delivery fee, when an errand is big or heavy. All of it is yours.

A Pasugo is the only job where you do the shopping, and the delivery fee only pays for the ride. So a big list earns more than a small one, because it takes you longer and is heavier to carry.

A Pasugo fee may be added on top of both. It is there for the part no distance or weight measures: on a Pasugo the money at the till is yours until the customer hands it back at the door, and a ₱3,000 errand puts far more of your own cash at risk than a ₱150 one.

How the Pasugo fee is worked out

You are paid the larger of two numbers — never both:

  • A base fee, so a small errand is still worth the ride
  • Or a percentage of what the goods cost, which takes over on a big one

You see the figure on the job card before you accept. Until you record what you actually spent it is worked out from the customer’s estimate, so if the percentage is what applies it moves with the real bill — down if it costs less, up if it costs more. If you are on the base fee it does not move. All of it is yours, with no commission taken.

How it adds up

  • The first 5 items and first 3 kg are included
  • Then ₱5 for each extra item
  • And ₱8 for each extra kilo
  • Counted by quantity — 10 cans is 10 items, not 1

What that looks like

  • 3 things, 2 kg — no extra
  • 8 things, 5 kg — ₱31
  • 12 things, 10 kg — ₱91
  • It still costs you one point, whatever the size

10 kg is the limit. A customer cannot post an errand heavier than that, because it has to go on your bike. The weight they picked is on the job, so check it before you accept — and if what is waiting at the shop is plainly heavier than what was declared, you do not have to take it.

Protect yourself

Before You Pay the Store

It is your own money on the counter. Spend ten seconds checking.

Why this matters more for you than on other apps. You pay the shop out of your own pocket and get it back at the door. If nobody is at that door, report it in the app before you leave — where you went to the address given, tried to reach the customer through the app and waited a reasonable time, PaspasFood pays you back what you spent on the goods, plus the delivery fee. That protection is why the checks below matter: it is the record the app keeps of you doing them that answers the question later.

🚩 Look twice when

  • The order is far bigger than a normal one for that store
  • The address is vague — no landmark, no house, just an area
  • The drop-off is somewhere you cannot picture at all
  • The customer does not answer when you ring before paying
  • They ask you to hand it to someone else, or to a different address
  • They push you to buy something extra and “settle it later”
  • Anything is urgent in a way that stops you checking

✅ What to do

  • Ring the customer before you pay. Their number appears on the job screen once the job is yours
  • Confirm the address out loud, and who will receive it
  • If nobody answers, wait — do not pay the store yet
  • Still no answer, or something feels wrong? Cancel and report it
  • If the shop charges more than the estimate, record the real amount — the app asks you to confirm and tells the customer straight away. Message them first if the difference is large
  • Keep the receipt for everything you buy

Backing out costs you the point, and that is fine. If you cancel a job yourself the point is not returned — it comes back only when the customer, the store or an admin cancels. But a point is ₱8 and a food advance is hundreds. If an order smells wrong, losing the ₱8 is the cheap way out. Do not carry on just because you already spent the point.

How to report one

Open Help Center from your profile, start a ticket and choose Report a Customer. It goes straight to the support team rather than sitting in the general queue.

Include the order number, the address given, and what happened. The reason you type when you cancel is kept on the order too, so write a real one — it is the record support reads later. We can only act on the accounts we are told about.

Do it well

Arriving In Good Condition

The food is someone’s dinner. It should reach them the way it left the kitchen.

📦

At the store

  • Check the bag against the order before you leave
  • Ask them to seal or tape the containers
  • Drinks bagged separately from hot food
  • Say so at the counter if something is missing — not at the door
🛵

On the road

  • Keep the bag upright — soup and sauce do not forgive a tilt
  • Nothing heavy stacked on top of the food
  • Slow over rough road. Two minutes is cheaper than a spill
  • Go straight there. No personal stops in the middle of a job
🙏

At the door

  • Greet them, hand the food over properly
  • Count the change in front of them
  • Take the proof-of-delivery photo
  • Thank them. You are the only part of PaspasFood they ever meet

If something does go wrong

A drink tips over, a container opens, the store got the order wrong. Tell the customer when you arrive and report it in the app — do not hand it over quietly and hope. A rider who says what happened keeps the customer. One who hides it loses them, and the next rider pays for that too.

Before your first shift

The Cash You Need to Start

You pay the store before anyone pays you.

Because you pay the store first and are repaid at the door, you need money in your pocket before you earn any. An order averages around ₱200, and you may hold two jobs at once — so plan for roughly ₱400 of working cash per shift, plus your points. A large order needs more.

This is a real requirement, and we would rather you knew it now than found out at a counter.

Choose the reason from the list. The app decides from that choice straight away and tells you whether your point came back before you leave the screen — you do not need to contact support for an ordinary problem. Typing your own reason under “Other” always uses the point, so pick the one that fits.

A point is used when you simply change your mind, because while you held that job nobody else could take it and the customer waited. It is not used when the job became impossible, or when stopping was the right thing to do — we would rather you stopped.

A returned point does not return money you already paid a store. That is what checking before you pay is for.

The agreement

Rules for Riders

Short, and all of them matter.

  • Hold no more than two active jobs at a time — food orders and Pasugo errands counted together. The app will not let you take a third until one is finished.
  • A photo is required as proof of delivery. You cannot complete a job without one.
  • Check the order against the slip before you pay the store. Once you have paid, a wrong or missing item is yours to resolve with them.
  • Do not open, consume, substitute or tamper with an order.
  • Do not buy alcohol, cigarettes, vapes, prescription medicines or anything illegal on a Pasugo, whatever the customer says. Refuse the errand and report it — you will not lose your point for refusing one.
  • Do not ask a customer for more than the total shown in the app, for any reason.
  • Keep your phone reachable while you hold a job. Customers are told they can contact you.
  • Treat customers and store staff courteously, whatever the situation. If a store treats you badly, report it — stores are held to the same standard under section 9 of the Terms.
  • Obey traffic law. Wear a helmet. No delivery is worth an injury.
  • Your account is yours. Do not let anyone else ride on it.

Losing your account

An account may be suspended or removed for:

  • Dishonesty about an order, a delivery or a payment.
  • Collecting or demanding more money than the app states.
  • Repeatedly cancelling accepted jobs without good reason.
  • Abusive, threatening or discriminatory behaviour towards anyone.
  • Tampering with orders, or delivering goods that are unsafe.
  • Letting someone else use your account.
  • Taking payment outside the platform for a job arranged on it.

If something goes wrong, contact support in the app — Profile → Help Center — or message our Facebook page. Report problems while they are fresh and include the order number; it begins with a #.

Onboarding

Before Your Meeting

Please bring your driver’s licence, a valid ID, and your phone with the app installed.

Come ready to discuss two things: how many points you want to start with, and how much working cash you can comfortably carry on a shift.

Write down anything here you disagree with or do not understand. The meeting is the right place to settle it.

One last word

These rules exist because real money moves between real people with no bank in the middle. You front your own cash; a store hands over food before being paid by a customer they never meet. That only works while everyone is straight with each other, and we would rather be clear about it now than apologetic about it later.
